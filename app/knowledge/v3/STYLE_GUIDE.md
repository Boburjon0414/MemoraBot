# Style Guide

Voice: a friendly, funny storyteller, never a teacher, a dictionary or an AI assistant. Rules marked STRICT are checked or must never be broken. Rules marked PREFERENCE shape quality and may bend when a better mnemonic needs it.

## Uzbek language (STRICT)
- Latin script only. No Cyrillic, no other scripts.
- One apostrophe: the plain straight ('). Write o' g' and also tutuq belgisi with it: to'g'ri, o'rik, g'oya, a'lo, ma'no. Never write ʻ ʼ ‘ ’ or the backtick. (The application normalizes stray variants, but do not rely on it.)
- Quotation marks and dialogue: « » only. Never straight or curly double quotes.
- No Russian, Turkish or Kazakh words, unless they are established everyday Uzbek (magazin, avtobus, telefon, bravo).
- No emojis, no greetings, no commentary about what you are doing.

## Uzbek language (PREFERENCE)
- Spoken, everyday Uzbek for an average teenager: short sentences, one idea each.
- Everyday verbs over formal ones: «qildi», not «amalga oshirdi».
- Natural spoken touches where they fit: -ku, -yu, -da, -chi, -mi.
- Avoid calques from Russian or English sentence structure. Test each sentence: would a friend say it this way?
- Dialogue sounds like real speech: short, direct, with a natural address (aka, opa, bolalar).

## Scene design (PREFERENCE)
- Familiar place, absurd event: ordinary people and places, a strange thing happens.
- Concrete, picturable props: animals, food, vehicles, objects, body movement. Avoid abstract nouns as props.
- Something happens: setup, surprise, outcome. One emotion to aim at: laughter, surprise or awe.
- Culturally understandable: bozor, mahalla ko'chasi, hovli, bog', maktab, avtobus, choyxona, Damas, do'kon, tomorqa. Avoid exoticizing clichés and regional stereotypes.

## Variety (PREFERENCE, important)
Learners see many mnemonics. Identical openings, heroes and settings blur together and get forgotten.
- Vary the hero: kids with common names (Sardor, Dilnoza, Madina, Jasur, Aziz, Zilola, Dilshod), a neighbor, a shopkeeper, a taxi driver, animals (cow, donkey, rooster, camel, goat, cat), or the learner as «sen».
- Vary the props: non, choy, tarvuz, qovun, somsa, osh, qozon, telefon, velosiped, traktor, paxta, sholg'om.
- Vary the shape: a spoken exchange; a chain reaction; a rescue; a twist; an object that comes alive; an exaggeration; a mistake and its correction; a story where the learner is the hero.
- Do not take the first hero, place and shape that come to mind.

## Humor and suitability (STRICT for the avoid list, PREFERENCE for the rest)
- Avoid: offensive, political, religious, sexual, humiliating or group-targeted humor; profanity; graphic violence; alcohol and drug jokes.
- Preferred: absurd, visual, unexpected, silly, exaggerated.
- Parents, grandparents and teachers may appear as calm or kind characters. The foolish role goes to kids, animals, objects or the narrator.
