# Examples

Few-shot demonstrations of the GOOD pattern versus the BAD pattern this
knowledge base defines. Match GOOD. Never produce BAD.

## GOOD — word: "kitchen"

```json
{
  "word": "kitchen",
  "meaning": "oshxona",
  "uzbek_pronunciation": "\"KICH-en\" — \"kichik\" so'ziga o'xshab eshitiladi",
  "mnemonic_story": "Onam oshxonaga kirdi-yu, juda **kichik** bir sichqoncha stol ostidan chiqib, to'g'ri idishga sakradi! Onam qattiq qichqirib, qopqoqni yopib qo'ydi. Kichkina mehmon esa idish ichida javrab, chiqib keta olmay qoldi.",
  "example_sentence": "My mother is cooking dinner in the kitchen.",
  "difficulty": "A1"
}
```

Why this works:

- Opens with the action itself, not "Imagine..." or "Suppose..." (Rule 10).
- 29 words — inside the 20-60 word target (Rule 7).
- "kichik" (small) is a real, common, natural Uzbek word that genuinely
  echoes "KICH-en" — not an invented, robotic-sounding mash-up.
- The sound-alike is staged as an actor *inside* the scene (a small mouse
  causing a scream and a slammed lid), not mentioned as a footnote.
- The meaning ("a small space where food is cooked") becomes obvious from
  the scene itself, without ever being stated as a definition.

## BAD — word: "kitchen" (do not produce this)

```json
{
  "mnemonic_story": "\"Kitchen\" so'zi \"oshxona\" degan ma'noni bildiradi. Bu so'z uy ichida ovqat pishiriladigan xona uchun ishlatiladi."
}
```

Why this fails:

- This is a dictionary definition restated in Uzbek — exactly the
  "textbook explanation" the Constitution and Memory Science documents say
  is forgotten within a day.
- No character, no action, no sound-alike woven in, nothing absurd or
  emotionally vivid.
- A learner who reads this has no more chance of recalling "kitchen" a week
  from now than before they read it.
