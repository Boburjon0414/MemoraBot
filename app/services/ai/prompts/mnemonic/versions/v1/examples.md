# Examples

These show the difference between a mnemonic that works and one that doesn't. Match the GOOD pattern; never the BAD one.

## GOOD — word: "kitchen"

- meaning: oshxona
- uzbek_pronunciation: "KICH-en" — "kichik" so'ziga o'xshab eshitiladi
- mnemonic_story: Onam oshxonaga kirib, juda **kichik** bir sichqonchani ko'rib qattiq qichqirib yubordi! U qo'liga bir tovoqni olib sichqonchaga tashladi, lekin sichqoncha muzlatgich ostiga sakrab qochib qutulib qoldi.
- Why it's good: "kichik" (small) is a real, common, natural-sounding Uzbek word that genuinely echoes "KICH-en." The story stages it as part of a noisy, ridiculous scene — a scream, a thrown pan, a narrow escape — not just mentioned in passing.

## BAD — word: "kitchen" (do not do this)

- mnemonic_story: "Kitchen" so'zi "oshxona" degan ma'noni bildiradi. Bu so'z uy ichida ovqat pishiriladigan xona uchun ishlatiladi.
- Why it's bad: this is just a dictionary definition restated in Uzbek. There is no story, no character, no sound-alike woven in, and nothing absurd or memorable — a learner forgets this by tomorrow.
